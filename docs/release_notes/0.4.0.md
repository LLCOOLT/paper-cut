Paper Cut 0.4.0 brings **versus battles**, Mario's side against Luigi's on one screen, online or against the computer, and **Paper Cut's own open world**, a randomizer where Rogueport and all eight chapters open in any order. Up to **four players** can play together, Luigi can join Mario as a second hero in **Brothers mode**, and Mario and his partners have **duo moves**. Online play moves into the game's own Start menu, with a room list, paper notes left for other players, leaderboards checked by replays and a weekly raid boss. It also adds **Master Quest**, a **Director's Cut** of what the game left out, things to do after the story, Peach and Bowser as heroes with moves of their own, costumes, paper looks for the picture, Lua mods, and much more. It plays the Japanese (G8MJ01), US (G8ME01) and European (G8MP01) versions of Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door, from the game's C decompilation, with no emulator.

**What's new**

*Versus battles*
- **Mario against Luigi:** Mario and his partners against Luigi and his, two players on one screen, in the Glitz Pit's ring or on seven battle stages from the chapters. The Start menu's Settings tab has a Versus battle button beside Photo mode.
- **The set-up:** a page in the game's windows, a side each and a cursor each with two controllers. Each side gets a level (its level-ups shared out among HP, FP and BP), boots and hammer, three partners and their rank, badges up to its BP, and up to six items. The rules: the stage, a turn timer (15, 30 or 60 seconds a command) and whether the luck badges count.
- **Eight stages:** the Glitz Pit, Petal Meadows, Hooktail Castle, Boggly Woods, Creepy Steeple, Keelhaul Key, the Palace of Shadow and the Rogueport Sewers, each one of the game's own battle stages with the audience and music of a battle there, named as the game names them.
- **The fight:** each side acts through the game's own command menu, with jumps, hammers, badge moves, partners, items, Star Powers and Tactics, and the defender guards and Superguards every hit on their own controller. As the curtain goes up, Mario and his partner run in from the left and strike a pose for the audience, then Luigi and his from the right. Luigi's HUD sits at the bottom of the screen. Afterwards Mario is back where he was, as he was: the fight only lends both sides what they were set up with.
- **Against the computer:** Luigi's side played by the game, easy, normal or hard. It weighs each move's damage against its FP, heals when low, swaps partners when one is down, uses the Star Powers once its Star Power has filled (heals, Earth Tremor, Art Attack, Supernova, Clock Out, Power Lift), Charges up when no hit would win yet, and Appeals while its Star Power isn't full.
- **The ladder:** Mario's side against a stronger computer Luigi on each rung, from two levels below Mario's, each fight on the next stage, until Mario falls. The most rungs won is kept.
- **Online:** in an online session the host is Mario and the guest Luigi. Both set up their side on the same page at once, and the fight plays in step like the rest of the game, on the host's stage.
- **Versus room:** a host's option (the launcher's and the Start menu's Online tab) for a session that's all about versus: every game of it opens straight on the set-up page as soon as a file is in play.

*Battles*
- **Battle grades** (Enhancements): each battle won graded D to S, from Stylish moves, Superguards, damage taken and turns; an A or an S pays a few coins more.
- **The Paper Cut badge set** (Enhancements): twelve new badges, each folded from two or three of the game's own (Glass Cannon, Paper Armor, Second Wind, Charmed Life...), sold by a Toad merchant by Rogueport's plaza.
- **Zess T.'s new dishes** (Enhancements): sixteen, from pairs of ingredients that made a Mistake before, to be found by trying.
- **The badge maker** (Enhancements): up to eight badges of your own, each up to four of the game's in one, its BP cost set fairly by formula, sold by the merchant and shared as codes.
- **Battle puzzles** (Modes): fifty of the game's battles with a fixed set-up and a goal ("win in 1 turn", without items, without being hurt...), from Goombas to the Shadow Queen, and one picked by the date for everyone each day. Losing one isn't a Game Over.
- **The Enemy maker** (Challenges): up to eight versions of the game's enemies, with more or less HP, attack and defence, a size, a colour, a move borrowed from another enemy, extra coins and a name and Tattle of your own, shared as codes and met in every battle with Use my enemies on.
- A save holding one of the new badges or dishes isn't readable by Dolphin or a GameCube any more: sell them or throw them away before taking the file there.

*Paper Cut's own open world (US)*
- **Chapters in any order:** a randomizer mode of Paper Cut's own, with no mod needed: after the prologue, Rogueport and all eight chapters open by what each way in needs (the Paper Curse, the Blimp Ticket, Yoshi, the Old Letter, the Train Ticket, the Ultra Hammer...), in whatever order the items allow. Turn on "Paper Cut's open world" on the Randomizer tab and start a new file.
- **Partners and key items shuffled,** with logic that makes sure every chapter can be reached. A partner is found as an item, named and drawn as the partner, and a partner's own scene gives whatever was placed there.
- **Each chapter has its own story:** a chapter goes in the game's own order inside, whichever chapters came before. Rogueport's own scenes (Frankly's, Zess T.'s, Don Pianta's Blimp Ticket...) follow the number of chapters done, and General White's hunt in chapter 7 asks in the other chapters' towns as in the game.
- **The goal:** 1 to 7 Crystal Stars, from any chapters, with a notice when it's reached. The Thousand-Year Door opens with that many, the Palace of Shadow lies behind it, and the Shadow Queen ends the game as always.
- **The file keeps its seed,** so it plays the same after a restart; other files stay as the game has them. The spoiler log has the playthrough, as before.
- **Online and in replays:** the host's randomizer settings go with the session's start, so a new file started there is the same open world in both games, and a replay keeps the recording's.
- **The daily seed** (Modes): the same open world for everyone each day, with two challenge rules drawn from the date and any three Crystal Stars for the goal, a clock of the file's own, and a results board for the day.
- **In the Start menu:** the randomizer's settings are a Randomizer category of the Settings tab, the seed typed on the game's own text page.

*Playing together*
- **Online in the Start menu:** a new Online tab in the game's own windows, on computers. It hosts, joins by code or address, watches, copies the code or invite link and starts the session, while you keep playing; when the host starts, both games start again into it. Codes and names are typed on a keyboard of its own, by controller, mouse or touch, with Paste. A launcher on one side plays with a Start menu on the other.
- **The room list:** a host can list their room (List my room, off by default), and the list shows the open rooms of your release and version, with how many are playing of how many can, to join with a press.
- **Three or four players:** player 3 walks the story's follower or a companion (another of Mario's partners, tagging along), player 4 a companion of their own; L or R picks another, and in battle the partner is played by whoever has it in the field. On one computer a third or fourth controller joins; online, Join as player 3 or 4 (the launcher's or the Start menu's Online tab) before the host starts. Replays keep all four controllers.
- **Brothers mode** (Multiplayer): Luigi joins Mario as a second hero, played by the second controller. In the field he walks, jumps and swings his hammer at blocks and switches, or follows Mario's steps; in battle he has his own turn, HP, FP and guards, sharing the items, badges and Star Power. Saves are untouched.
- **Duo moves** (Multiplayer, on in co-op and versus by default): a team attack for Mario and each partner, both action commands at once, and a second, stronger one at Ultra rank (Twin Bonk, Shell Stomp, Flutter Drop... Bonk Barrage, Shell Quake, Yoshi Rush...). Two players each press A; one player plays both with A and B.
- **Bros. moves:** the brothers' own team attacks with Brothers mode (Bros. Bounce, Bros. Hammer and Bros. Drop), in either brother's Jump or Hammer list, each brother's half on his own player's controller.
- **The raid boss:** one of six of the game's bosses each week (Bonetail, Hooktail, Magnus von Grapple 2.0, Gloomtail, Macho Grubba, Smorg), much stronger, for two to four players or one with Brothers mode's Luigi. Four phases: a shell only a duo move breaks, a fury stopped by everyone's Superguards, and attacks borrowed from other bosses. The week's clears go on a board, fewest turns first.
- **Paper notes:** set-phrase notes left on the ground for other players ("Hidden a Star Piece?"), shown in each player's own language. Others read them where they lie and rate them; each room keeps its best. Start menu > Online > Paper notes, and Multiplayer > Paper notes (on) to see them.
- **Leaderboards:** the boss rush and the Pit of 100 Trials, timed in the game's own frames. A run goes up with its replay only after your game has played it back to the same frame, and every game that watches an entry checks it again. Modes tab > Leaderboards.
- **Co-op pings:** either player points out a spot or an enemy with a paper arrow. Hold Ping (G, or Share) and aim with the C-stick, click the picture, or tap it. They work online too.
- **The Pit of 100 Trials in co-op:** a run from the first floor with the second player on the partner, the floor and the time on screen, the deepest floor and best time kept (Modes tab).

*Party modes*
- **Bingo** (Modes): a 5x5 card of goals from a seed, marked by itself as the file reaches them, with a tracker over the picture. In lockout, everyone types the same room code and each square goes to whoever finished it first.
- **The quiz party** (Modes): trivia for up to four players from the game's own data (what an item restores, an enemy's HP, a badge's BP...), answered with A, B, X or Y on each controller, in the game's windows with Quizmo's sounds.
- **Tattle cards** (Modes): the Tattle Log's enemies as cards with their own HP, Attack and Defense, a deck of eight to build, and best-of-five duels against three of Rogueport's Westside townsfolk (talk to them), a second controller, or the other player online.

*Play as*
- **Mario, Luigi, Peach or Bowser:** Play as Luigi is now a choice of four (Enhancements > Play as). Peach and Bowser come from their own models on your disc and take Mario's place in the field, in battles, the menus and the scenes, with their name in the text, their faces on the HUD and their steps. Mario plays their parts in their interludes. A saved Play as Luigi stays Luigi.
- **Every move their own:** Peach and Bowser have a version of each of Mario's moves made for them, from their hammer, Peach's jump and spin jump and Bowser's shell spin to Yoshi rides, lifting Bobbery, the Appeal, the story's poses and every battle move.

*Modes and challenges*
- **Master Quest** (Modes): the story's fights remixed from each area's own enemies, bigger and with the game's own statuses on their attacks, harder chapter by chapter from the prologue to chapter 8 and the Pit of 100 Trials. Make your own sets on the Challenges tab and share them as codes.
- **Chapter select:** any chapter's story played again on a finished file, with its Mario and the party the story has at that chapter's start; the file's story is put back after.
- **Remix modes:** each field enemy's fight drawn from its area's others, a badge draft at each chapter's title card, and a seed for both.
- **Hardcore files:** one life, no retries; the Game Over ends the file, and a recorded run is saved there to race.
- **Your own challenges:** rule sets of your own on the Challenges tab, named and shared as codes like settings presets.
- **Ghost races:** a replay keeps where Mario was, and racing it shows him see-through in the same rooms, with how far ahead or behind you are in each.
- **Level sync** (Gameplay): enemies below Mario's level brought up to it, after the credits and on New Game+ files, or always.
- **Console-accurate mode** (Gameplay): one switch that puts every setting that changes play back to the original game's, locked, and restores them when it's turned off.
- **Fun toggles** (Modes): big heads (on every character, grown about the neck), moon gravity, paper-thin from every side, Luigi's voice for Mario, spinning paper.
- **Chaos mode** (Modes): something happens every 30 seconds, in the field (giant Mario, flipped controls, coin rain...) and in battle (the audience's gifts and rocks). Offline only.
- **Trick practice** (Practice): warps to set-ups for known tricks, and spots of your own to go back to.
- **Splits for LiveSplit:** each speedrun attempt updates a `.lss` file in LiveSplit's own format, with the personal best and best segments.

*Director's Cut*
- **What the game left out, put back** (Modes > Director's Cut, off by default): content still in the game's code and on every disc that nothing ever uses, each with a page in the Director's Cut journal. It writes no new save flags.
- **Eight cut badges** sold by Dazzle: Mega Jump, Mega Smash, Mega Quake, Super Charge and Super Charge P, Triple Dip, Lucky Start P and Auto Command, with names and texts of Paper Cut's own.
- **Cut enemies and battles:** Boggly Woods' other set of enemy groups, Hooktail Castle's third Dull Bones, the Creepy Steeple's Swoopers and Boos, Keelhaul Key's four Fuzzies, and the Red Buzzy family in the Glitz Pit.
- **The sewers' three lost pipe rooms,** down the pipe Mario could only ever come out of, and the level-up's "Is this OK?" box.

*After the story*
- **The Paper Tower:** on a finished file, 30 floors of the game's corridor rooms with gates to get through in Paper or Tube mode, a Pit of 100 Trials battle every fifth floor, the day's climb for everyone or a seed of your own.
- **The trophy room:** a room under Rogueport that fills as the file plays: its Crystal Stars on the Door's pedestals, the bosses it has beaten as paper cut-outs, and a plaque for each achievement.
- **Area maps** (Settings > Area map): each area drawn from the game's own rooms and exits, with the rooms you've visited, the ways not yet taken and the Star Pieces and Shine Sprites still to find; on the second screen too.
- **Daily shop specials** (Modes): five shops each put one rare item on a shelf at 30% off, a different one each day, the same for everyone.
- **35 more achievements** for the new places and modes, 60 in all, each with a plaque in the trophy room.

*Looks*
- **Costumes:** paper versions of enemies' outfits for Mario (a Koopa's shell, a Boo's sheet, an X-Naut's hood and six more), made from the enemy's own model on your disc, unlocked by the file's Tattle Log.
- **Partner skins:** a character made with the character tools, drawn in a partner's place with its own animations, from the user folder's `skins` folder.
- **Stickers:** partners' faces, items and enemies, stuck anywhere in the world from the Wardrobe as your file meets them, and kept with the save file.
- **The Wardrobe dresses every partner wherever they're drawn:** in the field, in battle, on the Party page and in the scenes, with new parts (Goombella's helmet, Flurrie's dress, Bobbery's fuse, Vivian's hat and hair, Ms. Mowz's cape). Their faces on the HUD and their pictures in the menus take the colours too.
- **HUD themes:** Night, Forest, Ocean, Candy or High contrast, for the HP, FP and Star Power banners and every Start menu page together. A part with a colour of its own keeps it.
- **Paper looks** (Graphics): sharp text redrawn as a distance field, sharp at any resolution; smooth paper edges; paper shadows, shaped like each character's cut-out and falling away from the room's lamps; and paper physics (Subtle or Lively), cut-outs that lean, flutter and squash, the same at any refresh rate.
- **Two paper filters:** a cut-paper outline, and folding light (the picture on a folded sheet, a slow light moving over it).
- **Photo mode's filters, frames and depth of field:** nine filters (sepia, noir, cut paper...), a soft or strong depth of field sharp around Mario, and four frames (a paper border, a stamp, a film strip, a storybook's).
- **Pictures as you see them:** photo mode's pictures and F12 screenshots are saved at the window's size, with the effects on them, to Pictures > Paper Cut (and to Photos on an iPhone). The Journal has a Photos book to look through them.
- **Room transitions** (Display): doors and pipes turn the room away like a page, with the disc's own page animations (a pop-up book's page, a page flip, a fold).
- **The Tattle Log's models:** with an enemy's card open, the stick turns the enemy around, and L and R bring it in and out.
- **Widescreen bars that follow the camera:** in rooms whose empty space shows past a wall only as the camera nears it (Riverside Station, the X-Naut Fortress, Hooktail Castle...), the bars slide in only then.
- **The pop-up launcher:** as the launcher opens, its stage builds like a pop-up book, with Mario and his partners springing up along its edge (any button skips it).

*Settings and controls*
- **The whole settings page in the Start menu:** every section the full page had now has its page in the game's own windows too, from HDR and the mixer to setting up a controller, badge loadouts, the jukebox and sending saves (with the QR code).
- **L and R step through the Start menu's tabs,** as left and right do on the tab row.
- **Bindings:** a second controller button for each of the game's buttons (any button, or a stick or trigger direction), keys with Ctrl, Shift, Alt or Cmd held for Paper Cut's actions, and named profiles, one of which can load by itself for a kind of controller.
- **The mouse camera:** with the free camera on, the mouse turns it too (desktop), with a speed, an invert setting and a middle click to centre it.
- **Partner wheel** (Controls, on by default): hold the partner quick-swap for a ring of the partners, chosen by stick, keys, mouse or touch. A quick tap still swaps.
- **More haptics:** a tick as the Hammer's lights, Shell Toss's zone, the power and mash gauges and the other gauge and stick commands step on, and a pulse where they succeed; guards against attacks other than jumps are cued too, once the game has learned their timing.
- **The second screen wears badges and sorts:** A in its badge lists wears or takes off a badge, and its sort menus sort, online too. Its Bindings page can ask for a key or button.
- **Battery saver** (Graphics): on battery, or always, the picture is drawn 30 times a second while the game still runs at 60, at half the internal resolution.

*Saves, sound and Steam*
- **Every save file kept** (Saves > Save files, on by default): each save is also kept in a library, with a picture of where it was saved, its name, chapter, level and play time, and any of them goes back into one of the card's four files. The card stays Dolphin's `.gci`.
- **Settings per save file** (on by default): the difficulty, the Enhancements and the challenge rules saved with each file and set again when it loads.
- **Add to Steam** (General, Windows and Linux): Paper Cut in your Steam library as a non-Steam game, with artwork made from its own logo and controller set-ups for the Steam Deck and the usual controllers; a dated backup of Steam's list is kept first.
- **Headphones (3D)** (Audio > Speakers): the game's surround mix on headphones, as if from speakers around your head.
- **Sound from the DualSense** (Controls, on by default): on a DualSense connected by cable, the action commands' blips, the Superguard's clink and coins also play from the controller's speaker.

*Languages*
- **The games' own names:** the German, French, Spanish and Italian texts use the European release's names for partners, places, items, Star Points, the Tattle Log and HP, FP and BP, and the Japanese text the Japanese release's (スターストーン, ほしのかけら, ものしりリスト...), with the same words for the same settings throughout.
- **The achievements in every language:** their names and notes, and the pop-up, in German, French, Spanish, Italian and Japanese, as is every new feature.

*Mods*
- **Lua mods:** a mod can carry Lua scripts, with nothing to compile: the same mod runs on every platform and every version. Each runs in a sandbox of its own (no files, no network), sees the game's events (each frame, map, battle and hit) and has an API for battles, maps, the HUD and challenge rules. An error stops that mod alone; the Lua console is F4, and a mod in a folder reloads when its files change. Online, Lua mods run when every player has the same set. A guide (LUA_MODDING.md in the docs) and four example mods come with it.
- **Lua mods that keep up:** a mod's own values can be kept with save states and rewind (`ttyd.keep`), and a `state_load` event follows every load. Tables go through `pairs` in the same order on every computer, so mods stay in step online whatever their keys, and a runaway pattern stops the mod, not the game.
- **More for Lua:** battle units' names (the game's own, in the player's language), Master Quest's hooks to change field fights' enemies and their stats, and a looks library to choose partner skins and Mario's costume.
- **Code mods need rebuilding:** the Start menu's window data has room for one more tab (the Online tab), and the Journal's for one more book (Photos), so a code mod built against the 0.3.0 headers that reads the Start menu's `WindowWork` or the Journal's `WinLogState` must be built again against 0.4.0's. Paper Cut's new items take numbers from 0x160 on, after the game's own. Mods of files, textures, text, data and scripts aren't affected.

*Elsewhere*
- **What's new:** this list, in the game's own windows, at the first start after an update and in the Start menu (Settings > General > What's new).
- **Install updates automatically** (General, off by default): a new release downloaded and checked in the background and put in place at the next start, never during online play or a replay.
- **Crash reports sent with a click:** a filled-in GitHub issue, or a zip to attach, with your name taken out of its paths and the graphics adapter and last log lines added.

**Fixed**
- The built-in randomizer could move the Briefcase on the Excess Express, and then Zip Toad's scene couldn't be finished. It now stays where the game has it, so a seed shared from 0.3.0 places items differently.
- Character export refused about a hundred of the disc's models, Goombas and Koopas among them. Now 655 of the US disc's 656 come out and go back exactly.
- A memory card file made by Paper Cut had its empty files written in the PC's byte order, with checksums a GameCube or Dolphin wouldn't accept.
- A co-op replay could go out of step when played back with other controllers plugged in, or another window shape, than the recording had.
- A language pack's translation whose `%` codes differed from the English's could show the wrong text. It's now left out, and the pack's problems list says which.
- After a warp, a scene with a partner out where the story never has them showed a red "No messages". It now takes another partner's line.
- In widescreen, the battle hall's walls showed beside the stage, most of all as the curtain rose.
- At 21:9 and wider, some rooms showed the empty space past their walls as the camera neared an end.
- Past 16:9, the title screen's side curtains stopped short of the edges.
- Online, drawing Paper Cut's own Start menu tabs could take the game's random numbers and put the two games out of step.

**You need your own copy of the game:** a disc image (`.iso`, `.rvz`, `.gcm`...). Add it in the launcher.

**Known issues**
- Versus battles: with one controller only, the side being hit can't guard. The computer's Luigi leaves Showstopper and the moves played only for their statuses to players.
- Paper Cut's open world and the daily seed are the US version's only, and the open world has been checked by its tests more than played: chapters 3 to 8 haven't been played through in it yet. Some things look odd: when a partner's own scene gives an item instead, the lines after it are spoken by the partner who is out; a partner found before their chapter shows in that chapter's scenes too; Rogueport's scenes follow how many chapters are done, so they may name another chapter's star; the black chests give what was placed there without showing it; Keelhaul Key's wedding ring errand after chapter 5 doesn't play; during General White's hunt the towns he's asked about show their later selves; and the Thousand-Year Door's scene may show all seven stars whatever the goal.
- Brothers mode: Luigi's hammer doesn't start a battle with a first strike, he doesn't talk to people, and his HP and FP aren't shown in the field.
- Three or four players have been played by address on one computer; through a room they're new, and recording a replay with players 3 and 4 hasn't been tried yet. Players 3 and 4's input comes a little later than the first two's.
- For Peach and Bowser, the level-up's BP panel is still Mario's, collision, speed and jump are Mario's, and the credits draw Bowser at his full size.
- A save holding one of the new badges or dishes (the badge set's, the badge maker's, Zess T.'s new dishes) can't go to Dolphin or a GameCube until they're sold or thrown away.
- The Start menu's Online tab isn't on phones and tablets, whose apps can't start themselves again: use the launcher there. Leaderboard runs are recorded on computers only (phones can look at the boards).
- The room list, paper notes, leaderboards, lockout bingo, the daily seed's and the raid's boards, and joining a room as player 3 or 4 go through Paper Cut's relay, which is free and has a daily allowance shared by everyone it carries: when it's used up it says so until midnight UTC. Paper notes are off during online co-op and replays, and ghost races are local only.
- Online play has been tried mostly on one computer and one home network; between two homes, and with phones, it's still new. Minimizing the window pauses that player's game, and the session ends after 15 seconds.
- Online, save states, fast-forward, frame advance, rewind, the Practice tab's editors, warps, chaos mode and the Twitch chat are off, and so are chapter select, the Paper Tower, the trophy room, daily shop specials, battle puzzles, the Enemy maker, the quiz party, the Westside's card players, partner skins and stickers. Costumes are the host's for everyone.
- Much of 0.4.0 has been checked by scripted runs more than by hand: the Paper Tower's gates in Paper and Tube mode (Skip this floor moves on if one won't let Mario through), the quiz with four controllers, a Tattle-card duel online, Director's Cut's badges in battle, the save library's buttons, the area maps, the pings, the partner wheel's hold, the mouse camera and the second controller buttons are still to be played properly.
- Some of the Start menu's help lines take three lines: the window shows two, with an arrow for the rest.
- With sharp text on, a texture pack's font isn't used: turn it off to see the pack's.
- Add to Steam has been tried against a test copy of Steam's folders, not yet a real Steam library or a Steam Deck. Headphones (3D) and surround haven't been listened to on real headphones or speakers, and the DualSense's speaker, haptics and light haven't been tried on a real controller.
- The second screen hasn't run on a real dual-screen device yet.
- In mirror mode, signs and text that are part of the 3D world read backwards.
- Photo mode works in the field only, not in battle and not online.
- Read text aloud has only been run on Windows; the Mac, iPhone, Android and Linux voices are untried. Exclusive full screen hasn't been tried on a second monitor or a mixed-refresh setup.
- The translations are Paper Cut's own, not yet checked by native speakers.
- Nothing is code-signed. Windows SmartScreen warns on first run (More info > Run anyway). On macOS, the first time it's blocked: try to open it, then System Settings > Privacy & Security > Open Anyway. The iOS app must be signed by a sideloading tool.
- Linux and the Steam Deck haven't been played on yet, and the Android apps haven't run on a device.
