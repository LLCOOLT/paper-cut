Paper Cut 0.4.0 brings **versus battles**, Mario's side against Luigi's on one screen, online or against the computer, and **Paper Cut's own open world**, a randomizer where Rogueport and the first five chapters open in any order. Online play moves into the game's own Start menu, with a room list, paper notes left for other players and leaderboards checked by replays. It also adds Peach and Bowser as heroes, costumes, partner skins and stickers, paper looks for the picture, Lua mods, and much more. It plays the Japanese (G8MJ01), US (G8ME01) and European (G8MP01) versions of Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door, from the game's C decompilation, with no emulator.

**What's new**

*Versus battles*
- **Mario against Luigi:** Mario and his partners against Luigi and his, in the Glitz Pit's ring, two players on one screen. The Start menu's Settings tab has a Versus battle button beside Photo mode.
- **The set-up:** a page in the game's windows, a side each and a cursor each with two controllers. Each side gets a level (its level-ups shared out among HP, FP and BP), boots and hammer, three partners and their rank, badges up to its BP, and up to six items. The rules: a turn timer (15, 30 or 60 seconds a command) and whether the luck badges count.
- **The fight:** each side acts through the game's own command menu, with jumps, hammers, badge moves, partners, items, Star Powers and Tactics, and the defender guards and Superguards every hit on their own controller. Luigi's HUD sits at the bottom of the screen. Afterwards Mario is back where he was, as he was: the fight only lends both sides what they were set up with.
- **Against the computer:** Luigi's side played by the game, easy, normal or hard. It weighs each move's damage against its FP, heals when low and swaps partners when one is down.
- **The ladder:** Mario's side against a stronger computer Luigi on each rung, fight after fight, until Mario falls. The most rungs won is kept.
- **Online:** in an online session the host is Mario and the guest Luigi. Both set up their side on the same page at once, and the fight plays in step like the rest of the game.

*Paper Cut's own open world (US)*
- **Chapters in any order:** a randomizer mode of Paper Cut's own, with no mod needed: after the prologue, Rogueport and chapters 1 to 5 open by what each way in needs (the Paper Curse, the Blimp Ticket, Yoshi, the Old Letter...), in whatever order the items allow. Turn on "Paper Cut's open world" on the Randomizer tab and start a new file.
- **Partners and key items shuffled,** with logic that makes sure every chapter can be reached. A partner is found as an item, named and drawn as the partner, and a partner's own scene gives whatever was placed there.
- **Each chapter has its own story:** a chapter goes in the game's own order inside, whichever chapters came before. Rogueport's own scenes (Frankly's, Zess T.'s, Don Pianta's Blimp Ticket...) follow the number of chapters done.
- **The goal:** 1 to 5 Crystal Stars, from any chapters, with a notice when it's reached.
- **The file keeps its seed,** so it plays the same after a restart; other files stay as the game has them. The spoiler log has the playthrough, as before.
- **In the Start menu:** the randomizer's settings are a Randomizer category of the Settings tab, the seed typed on the game's own text page.

*Playing together*
- **Online in the Start menu:** a new Online tab in the game's own windows, on computers. It hosts, joins by code or address, watches, copies the code or invite link and starts the session, while you keep playing; when the host starts, both games start again into it. Codes and names are typed on a keyboard of its own, by controller, mouse or touch, with Paste. A launcher on one side plays with a Start menu on the other.
- **The room list:** a host can list their room (List my room, off by default), and the list shows the open rooms of your release and version, with how many are playing online, to join with a press.
- **Paper notes:** set-phrase notes left on the ground for other players ("Hidden a Star Piece?"), shown in each player's own language. Others read them where they lie and rate them; each room keeps its best. Start menu > Online > Paper notes, and Multiplayer > Paper notes (on) to see them.
- **Leaderboards:** the boss rush and the Pit of 100 Trials, timed in the game's own frames. A run goes up with its replay only after your game has played it back to the same frame, and every game that watches an entry checks it again. Modes tab > Leaderboards.
- **Co-op pings:** either player points out a spot or an enemy with a paper arrow. Hold Ping (G, or Share) and aim with the C-stick, click the picture, or tap it. They work online too.
- **The Pit of 100 Trials in co-op:** a run from the first floor with the second player on the partner, the floor and the time on screen, the deepest floor and best time kept (Modes tab).

*Play as*
- **Mario, Luigi, Peach or Bowser:** Play as Luigi is now a choice of four (Enhancements > Play as). Peach and Bowser come from their own models on your disc, with Mario's moves, and take his place in the field, in battles, the menus and the scenes, with their name in the text, their faces on the HUD and their steps. Mario plays their parts in their interludes. A saved Play as Luigi stays Luigi.

*Modes and challenges*
- **Chapter select:** any chapter's story played again on a finished file, with its Mario; the file's story is put back after.
- **Remix modes:** each field enemy's fight drawn from its area's others, a badge draft at each chapter's title card, and a seed for both.
- **Hardcore files:** one life, no retries; the Game Over ends the file, and a recorded run is saved there to race.
- **Your own challenges:** rule sets of your own on the Challenges tab, named and shared as codes like settings presets.
- **Ghost races:** a replay keeps where Mario was, and racing it shows him see-through in the same rooms, with how far ahead or behind you are in each.
- **Level sync** (Gameplay): enemies below Mario's level brought up to it, after the credits and on New Game+ files, or always.
- **Console-accurate mode** (Gameplay): one switch that puts every setting that changes play back to the original game's, locked, and restores them when it's turned off.
- **Fun toggles** (Modes): big heads, moon gravity, paper-thin from every side, Luigi's voice for Mario, spinning paper.
- **Chaos mode** (Modes): something happens every 30 seconds, in the field (giant Mario, flipped controls, coin rain...) and in battle (the audience's gifts and rocks). Offline only.
- **Trick practice** (Practice): warps to set-ups for known tricks, and spots of your own to go back to.
- **Splits for LiveSplit:** each speedrun attempt updates a `.lss` file in LiveSplit's own format, with the personal best and best segments.

*Looks*
- **Costumes:** paper versions of enemies' outfits for Mario (a Koopa's shell, a Boo's sheet, an X-Naut's hood and six more), made from the enemy's own model on your disc, unlocked by the file's Tattle Log.
- **Partner skins:** a character made with the character tools, drawn in a partner's place with its own animations, from the user folder's `skins` folder.
- **Stickers:** partners' faces, items and enemies, stuck anywhere in the world from the Wardrobe as your file meets them, and kept with the save file.
- **The Wardrobe dresses every partner wherever they're drawn:** in the field, in battle, on the Party page and in the scenes, with new parts (Goombella's helmet, Flurrie's dress, Bobbery's fuse, Vivian's hat and hair, Ms. Mowz's cape). Their faces on the HUD and their pictures in the menus take the colours too.
- **HUD themes:** Night, Forest, Ocean, Candy or High contrast, for the HP, FP and Star Power banners and every Start menu page together. A part with a colour of its own keeps it.
- **Paper looks** (Graphics): sharp text redrawn as a distance field, sharp at any resolution; smooth paper edges; paper shadows, shaped like each character's cut-out and falling away from the room's lamps; and paper physics (Subtle or Lively), cut-outs that lean, flutter and squash.
- **Two paper filters:** a cut-paper outline, and folding light (the picture on a folded sheet, a slow light moving over it).
- **Photo mode's filters, frames and depth of field:** nine filters (sepia, noir, cut paper...), a soft or strong depth of field sharp around Mario, and four frames (a paper border, a stamp, a film strip, a storybook's).
- **Pictures as you see them:** photo mode's pictures and F12 screenshots are saved at the window's size, with the effects on them, to Pictures > Paper Cut (and to Photos on an iPhone). The Journal has a Photos book to look through them.
- **Room transitions** (Display): doors and pipes turn the room away like a page, with the disc's own page animations (a pop-up book's page, a page flip, a fold).
- **The Tattle Log's models:** with an enemy's card open, the stick turns the enemy around, and L and R bring it in and out.
- **Widescreen bars that follow the camera:** in rooms whose empty space shows past a wall only as the camera nears it (Riverside Station, the X-Naut Fortress, Hooktail Castle...), the bars slide in only then.
- **The pop-up launcher:** as the launcher opens, its stage builds like a pop-up book, with Mario and his partners springing up along its edge (any button skips it).

*Settings and controls*
- **The whole settings page in the Start menu:** every section the full page had now has its page in the game's own windows too, from HDR and the mixer to setting up a controller, badge loadouts, the jukebox and sending saves (with the QR code).
- **L and R step through the Start menu's tabs,** as left and right do on the tab row.
- **Bindings:** a second controller button for each of the game's buttons (any button, or a stick or trigger direction), keys with Ctrl, Shift, Alt or Cmd held for Paper Cut's actions, and named profiles, one of which can load by itself for a kind of controller.
- **The mouse camera:** with the free camera on, the mouse turns it too (desktop), with a speed, an invert setting and a middle click to centre it.
- **Partner wheel** (Controls, on by default): hold the partner quick-swap for a ring of the partners, chosen by stick, keys, mouse or touch. A quick tap still swaps.
- **More haptics:** a tick as the Hammer's lights, Shell Toss's zone, the power and mash gauges and the other gauge and stick commands step on, and a pulse where they succeed; guards against attacks other than jumps are cued too, once the game has learned their timing.
- **The second screen wears badges and sorts:** A in its badge lists wears or takes off a badge, and its sort menus sort, online too. Its Bindings page can ask for a key or button.
- **Battery saver** (Graphics): on battery, or always, the picture is drawn 30 times a second while the game still runs at 60, at half the internal resolution.

*Mods*
- **Lua mods:** a mod can carry Lua scripts, with nothing to compile: the same mod runs on every platform and every version. Each runs in a sandbox of its own (no files, no network), sees the game's events (each frame, map, battle and hit) and has an API for battles, maps, the HUD and challenge rules. An error stops that mod alone; the Lua console is F4, and a mod in a folder reloads when its files change. Online, Lua mods run when every player has the same set. A guide (LUA_MODDING.md in the docs) and three example mods come with it.
- **Code mods need rebuilding:** the Start menu's window data has room for one more tab (the Online tab), so a code mod built against the 0.3.0 headers that reads the Start menu's `WindowWork` must be built again against 0.4.0's. Mods of files, textures, text, data and scripts aren't affected.

*Elsewhere*
- **What's new:** this list, in the game's own windows, at the first start after an update and in the Start menu (Settings > General > What's new).
- **Install updates automatically** (General, off by default): a new release downloaded and checked in the background and put in place at the next start, never during online play or a replay.
- **Crash reports sent with a click:** a filled-in GitHub issue, or a zip to attach, with your name taken out of its paths and the graphics adapter and last log lines added.

**Known issues**
<!-- coordinator: revise after the test pass -->
- Versus battles are new: the Glitz Pit's ring is the only stage, and the computer's Luigi doesn't use Star Powers or Charge yet. With one controller only, the side being hit can't guard.
- Paper Cut's open world is the US version's only, and has been checked by its tests more than played. Some things look odd: when a partner's own scene gives an item instead, the lines after it are spoken by the partner who isn't there; a partner found before their chapter shows in that chapter's scenes too; Rogueport's scenes follow how many chapters are done, so they may name another chapter's star; the black chests give what was placed there without showing it.
- The Start menu's Online tab isn't on phones and tablets, whose apps can't start themselves again: use the launcher there. Leaderboard runs are recorded on computers only (phones can look at the boards).
- The room list, paper notes and leaderboards go through Paper Cut's relay, which is free and has a daily allowance shared by everyone it carries. Paper notes are off during online co-op and replays, and ghost races are local only.
- Online, save states, fast-forward, frame advance, rewind, the Practice tab's editors, warps, chaos mode and the Twitch chat are off.
- Peach and Bowser use Mario's moves. For them the pause menu's first tab and the level-up's BP panel are still Mario's, and the credits draw Bowser at his full size.
- With sharp text on, a texture pack's font isn't used: turn it off to see the pack's.
- The second screen hasn't run on a real dual-screen device yet.
- The translations are Paper Cut's own, not yet checked by native speakers.
- Nothing is code-signed. Windows SmartScreen warns on first run (More info > Run anyway). On macOS, the first time it's blocked: try to open it, then System Settings > Privacy & Security > Open Anyway. The iOS app must be signed by a sideloading tool.
- Linux and the Steam Deck haven't been played on yet, and the Android apps haven't run on a device.
