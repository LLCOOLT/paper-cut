Paper Cut 0.4.0 brings **versus battles**, Mario's side against Luigi's on one screen, online or against a computer that plays every Star Power, and **Paper Cut's own open world**, a randomizer where Rogueport and all eight chapters open in any order. **Luigi, Peach or Bowser** can take Mario's place for the whole game, name and all. The title screen gets a **menu of its own** (Play, Modes, Online, Settings), and the new **Modes** page gathers an action-command dojo, the dungeon master, the boss rush, party games and more. Up to **four players** can play together, Luigi can join Mario as a second hero in **Brothers mode**, and Mario and his partners have **duo moves**. Online play moves into the game's own Start menu, with a room list, paper notes, leaderboards checked by replays and a weekly raid boss. It also adds **Master Quest**, a **Director's Cut** of what the game left out, things to do after the story, costumes, paper looks for the picture, button icons for your controller, a new launcher, Lua mods, and much more. It plays the Japanese (G8MJ01), US (G8ME01) and European (G8MP01) versions of Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door, from the game's C decompilation, with no emulator.

**Highlights**

- Versus battles against a friend or the computer.
- Paper Cut's open world: chapters in any order (US).
- Play as Luigi, Peach or Bowser, all the way through.
- A title menu: Play, Modes, Online and Settings.

**What's new**

*The title menu and the launcher*
- **A title menu:** after Press Start, four buttons along the bottom of the title screen, in the Start menu's own look, with the logo and the whole cast still in view: **Play** (your files, as before), **Modes**, **Online** and **Settings**. Stick, D-pad, keys, mouse or a tap move through them. Settings and Online open the Start menu's own tabs over the title, with no file loaded. General > Title menu turns it off, for Start straight to the files as in the original.
- **Modes from the title:** party games and battles (versus battles, the raid boss, the quiz party, Tattle cards, the dungeon master, the boss rush with a loadout, battle puzzles, the dojo and the fun toggles) on a file of their own that is never saved, with the Tattle Log lent for card decks. When the mode is over, you're back at the title.
- **The launcher, dressed as the game's menus:** paper windows on a stage, the Start menu's coloured tabs, a white glove that glides to whatever is chosen, torn-paper buttons, a page turn between tabs and soft paper sounds (General > Launcher sounds). Each disc shows its own banner with a JP, US or EU tag. A short window keeps Play in sight, and on a phone the tabs shrink to fit.
- **A cut-paper theatre:** the launcher's stage is redrawn in layers of paper: pleated red curtains tied back with gold tasselled ropes, a valance of swags and fringe, a paper moon and stars on strings, clouds, and stage boards with footlights. The curtains and stars sway gently (they keep still when your system's animation effects are off), and the pop-up opening builds it all in turn.
- **The Paper Cut wordmark and a new icon:** the title on the launcher is a crafted cut-paper wordmark, and Paper Cut's icon is a gold paper star snipped out by scissors, on every platform (with Android's themed icons too).
- **Launcher look** (General): Paper Cut's own look (the default, the same before any disc is added), or a trial of the game's own art, made from your disc.

*Versus battles*
- **Mario against Luigi:** Mario and his partners against Luigi and his, two players on one screen, in the Glitz Pit's ring or on seven battle stages from the chapters. Start one from the Modes page (the Start menu's Settings tab, or the title menu's Modes).
- **The set-up:** a page in the game's windows, a side each and a cursor each with two controllers. Each side gets a level (its level-ups shared out among HP, FP and BP), boots and hammer, three partners and their rank, badges up to its BP, and up to six items. The rules: the stage, a turn timer (15, 30 or 60 seconds a command), whether the luck badges count, and whether Showstopper knocks out.
- **Fair-play rules:** by default Showstopper takes a sixth to a third of each foe's HP instead of knocking them out (turn on "Showstopper knocks out" for the story's rule), and the luck badges are off. With one controller between two players, the side being hit guards on it.
- **Eight stages:** the Glitz Pit, Petal Meadows, Hooktail Castle, Boggly Woods, Creepy Steeple, Keelhaul Key, the Palace of Shadow and the Rogueport Sewers, each one of the game's own battle stages with the audience and music of a battle there.
- **The fight:** each side acts through the game's own command menu, with jumps, hammers, badge moves, partners, items, Star Powers and Tactics, and the defender guards and Superguards every hit on their own controller. As the curtain goes up, Mario and his partner run in from the left and strike a pose, then Luigi and his from the right. Luigi's HUD sits at the bottom of the screen. Afterwards Mario is back where he was, as he was.
- **Luigi's Star Powers face Mario's side:** Art Attack, Clock Out, Earth Tremor and the rest hit the other side whichever brother plays them, his Stop lasts as long as Mario's, and his Sweet Treat shows his own face.
- **Against the computer:** Luigi's side played by the game, easy, normal or hard. It weighs each move's damage, FP and statuses, heals when low, swaps partners when one is down, Charges, Appeals, and plays every Star Power's own action command (Art Attack's drawing, Supernova's mashing, Showstopper's buttons), as well as its difficulty allows.
- **The ladder:** Mario's side against a stronger computer Luigi on each rung, each fight on the next stage, until Mario falls. The most rungs won is kept.
- **Online:** the host is Mario and the guest Luigi. Both set up their side on the same page at once ("You: Mario's side" on each screen, the other player's cursor in green), and the fight plays in step on the host's stage. **Versus room** (the launcher's and the Start menu's Online tab) opens every game of the session straight on the set-up page.

*Modes*
- **A page for every mode:** the Start menu's Settings tab has a Modes page of picture buttons, one per group: Party games, Battles, After the story, Every day, Story twists and Just for fun. Each opens its modes in the game's own book, with their options and their start, so a controller is all you need. The pictures are made from the game's own icons on your disc.
- **The action-command dojo** (Battles): a practice battle on the Glitz Pit's ring against one of the Pit's fighters, who never falls. Each action command, guard, Superguard and Stylish move is told in frames ("3 frames early", "Perfect!") with the timing window drawn as a bar. Mario can borrow every move for it, and nothing done there stays.
- **Timing assist by command type** (Accessibility): widen only the kinds of command you struggle with, set by hand or from the dojo's suggestions. **Action command timing in every battle** shows the dojo's window in any fight.
- **The dungeon master** (Multiplayer): another player plays the enemies in the story's battles, picking each enemy's move and target from its own moves, with the damage each would do. Player 2, 3 or 4, at home or online.
- **The boss rush, refights and the endless Pit** start from the Modes page too, refights with a choice of boss, and every mode waits for the Start menu to close.

*Battles*
- **The battle recap** (Enhancements, Short by default): after each battle won, a card in the game's own look with the damage dealt and taken, the best hit and the turns (Full adds Superguards, Stylish moves, FP, items and Star Points), the grade stamped on it. It never holds the game up; A or B puts it away.
- **Records:** a new Journal book of lifetime stats, for this file and for every file on the device: time played, distance walked, jumps and hammer swings, coins, enemies defeated, battles won and lost, Superguards and more.
- **Battle grades** (Enhancements): each battle won graded D to S, from Stylish moves, Superguards, damage taken and turns; an A or an S pays a few coins more.
- **The Paper Cut badge set** (Enhancements): twelve new badges, each folded from two or three of the game's own (Glass Cannon, Paper Armor, Second Wind, Charmed Life...), sold by a Toad merchant by Rogueport's plaza.
- **Zess T.'s new dishes** (Enhancements): sixteen, from pairs of ingredients that made a Mistake before, to be found by trying.
- **The badge maker** (Enhancements): up to eight badges of your own, each up to four of the game's in one, its BP cost set fairly by formula, sold by the merchant and shared as codes.
- **Battle puzzles** (Modes): fifty of the game's battles with a fixed set-up and a goal ("win in 1 turn", without items, without being hurt...), from Goombas to the Shadow Queen, and one picked by the date for everyone each day. Losing one isn't a Game Over.
- **The Enemy maker** (Challenges): up to eight versions of the game's enemies, with more or less HP, attack and defence, a size, a colour, a move borrowed from another enemy, extra coins and a name and Tattle of your own, shared as codes.

*Paper Cut's own open world (US)*
- **Chapters in any order:** a randomizer mode of Paper Cut's own, with no mod needed: after the prologue, Rogueport and all eight chapters open by what each way in needs (the Paper Curse, the Blimp Ticket, Yoshi, the Old Letter, the Train Ticket, the Ultra Hammer...), in whatever order the items allow. Turn on "Paper Cut's open world" on the Randomizer page and start a new file.
- **Partners and key items shuffled,** with logic that makes sure every chapter can be reached. A partner is found as an item, named and drawn as the partner, in your game's language.
- **Each chapter has its own story:** a chapter goes in the game's own order inside, whichever chapters came before.
- **The goal:** 1 to 7 Crystal Stars, from any chapters, with a notice when it's reached. The Thousand-Year Door shows the stars you have and opens with that many, and the Shadow Queen ends the game as always.
- **Nothing gets lost:** black chests, Shine Sprite blocks and coin blocks give what the seed put there, and a key item that sinks in water or falls through a floor comes back where it appeared.
- **The file keeps its seed,** so it plays the same after a restart. Online, the host's randomizer settings go with the session's start; replays keep the recording's.
- **The daily seed** (Modes): the same open world for everyone each day, with two challenge rules drawn from the date, any three Crystal Stars for the goal, and a results board for the day.

*Play as*
- **Luigi, Peach or Bowser, all the way:** Play as (Gameplay) is a choice of four, and the pick takes Mario's place everywhere: the field, battles, the scenes, the credits, the HUD's faces, the level-up's BP panel, the pause menu's tab (in the game's lettering, in Japanese too) and the name in every text, the game's and Paper Cut's own. Mario plays their parts in Peach's and Bowser's interludes.
- **Moves of their own:** Peach and Bowser come from their own models on your disc, with a version of every one of Mario's moves: Peach's two-handed hammer and overhead throw, Bowser's shell spin and ground pound, Bowser astride Yoshi and lifting Bobbery over his head, and a back view of their own.
- **The Wardrobe dresses the pick:** Luigi's cap and overalls (and his Mario, Wario, Waluigi and Fire Luigi looks), Peach's dress, crown, hair and gloves, Bowser's shell, hair, skin and horns, each saved apart. Costumes fit every pick.
- Brothers mode turns the pick off while it's on, since Luigi is beside Mario there. Online, the host's pick is everyone's.

*Playing together*
- **Online in the Start menu:** an Online tab in the game's own windows, on computers. Host a room, join by code or address, watch, copy the code or invite link and start the session while you keep playing; when the host starts, every game starts again into it. Codes and names are typed by controller, keyboard, mouse or touch, with Paste.
- **Lobby news:** while you wait, news shows over the picture so the host can play on with the menu closed: the other player is here, has left, or can't play (another version or other mods: the room stays open with the same code, and the reason is plain).
- **Waits you can see:** when the game holds for another player or a reconnect, a notice over the picture says so ("Waiting for...", "...minimized the game.", "Reconnecting..."), even in full screen. Any player can leave a session from Multiplayer > Online play, and the others play on.
- **The room list:** a host can list their room (List my room, off by default), and the list shows the open rooms of your release and version, to join with a press.
- **Three or four players:** player 3 walks the story's follower or a companion, player 4 a companion of their own; in battle the partner is played by whoever has it. On one computer a third or fourth controller joins; online, Join as player 3 or 4. Replays keep all four controllers.
- **Brothers mode** (Multiplayer): Luigi joins Mario as a second hero, played by the second controller. In the field he walks, jumps, swings his hammer (a first strike on an enemy), talks to people and shows his HP and FP; in battle he has his own turn, HP, FP and guards. Saves are untouched.
- **Duo moves** (Multiplayer, on in co-op and versus by default): a team attack for Mario and each partner, both action commands at once, and a stronger one at Ultra rank (Twin Bonk, Shell Stomp, Flutter Drop... Bonk Barrage, Shell Quake, Yoshi Rush...). **Bros. moves** are the brothers' own in Brothers mode.
- **The raid boss:** one of six of the game's bosses each week, much stronger, for two to four players or one with Brothers mode's Luigi, in four phases. The week's clears go on a board.
- **Paper notes:** set-phrase notes left on the ground for other players ("Hidden a Star Piece?"), shown in each player's own language and rated; each room keeps its best.
- **Leaderboards:** the boss rush and the Pit of 100 Trials, timed in the game's own frames. A run goes up with its replay only after your game has played it back to the same frame.
- **Co-op pings:** either player points out a spot or an enemy with a paper arrow. Hold Ping and aim with the C-stick, click the picture, or tap it.
- **The Pit of 100 Trials in co-op:** a run from the first floor with the second player on the partner.
- **More modes online:** chapter select, the Paper Tower, the trophy room, battle puzzles, the quiz party, Tattle-card duels, daily shop specials, chaos mode and the Enemy maker's enemies now play in online sessions too, started from the Start menu in every game at once.

*Party modes*
- **Bingo** (Modes): a 5x5 card of goals from a seed, marked by itself as the file reaches them. In lockout, everyone types the same room code and each square goes to whoever finished it first.
- **The quiz party** (Modes): trivia for up to four players from the game's own data, answered with A, B, X or Y on each controller, with Quizmo's sounds.
- **Tattle cards** (Modes): the Tattle Log's enemies as cards, a deck of eight to build, and best-of-five duels against three of Rogueport's Westside townsfolk, a second controller, or the other player online.

*Story twists and challenges*
- **Master Quest** (Modes): the story's fights remixed from each area's own enemies, bigger and with the game's own statuses on their attacks, harder chapter by chapter to chapter 8 and the Pit. Make your own sets and share them as codes.
- **Chapter select:** any chapter's story played again on a finished file, with the party the story has at its start; the file's story is put back after.
- **Remix modes:** each field enemy's fight drawn from its area's others, and a badge draft at each chapter's title card.
- **Hardcore files:** one life, no retries; the Game Over ends the file.
- **Your own challenges:** rule sets of your own on the Challenges tab, named and shared as codes.
- **Ghost races:** a replay keeps where Mario was, and racing it shows him see-through in the same rooms, with how far ahead or behind you are.
- **Level sync** (Gameplay): enemies below Mario's level brought up to it, after the credits and on New Game+ files, or always.
- **Console-accurate mode** (Gameplay): one switch that puts every setting that changes play back to the original game's, locked.
- **Fun toggles** (Modes): big heads, moon gravity, paper-thin from every side, Luigi's voice for Mario, spinning paper.
- **Chaos mode** (Modes): something happens every 30 seconds, in the field (giant Mario, flipped controls, coin rain...) and in battle.
- **Trick practice** (Practice): warps to set-ups for known tricks, and spots of your own to go back to. **Splits for LiveSplit:** each speedrun attempt updates a `.lss` file.

*Director's Cut*
- **What the game left out, put back** (Story twists > Director's Cut, off by default): content still in the game's code and on every disc that nothing ever uses, each with a page in the Director's Cut journal. It writes no new save flags.
- **Eight cut badges** sold by Dazzle: Mega Jump, Mega Smash, Mega Quake, Super Charge and Super Charge P, Triple Dip, Lucky Start P and Auto Command.
- **Cut enemies and battles:** Boggly Woods' other enemy groups, Hooktail Castle's third Dull Bones, the Creepy Steeple's Swoopers and Boos, Keelhaul Key's four Fuzzies, and the Red Buzzy family in the Glitz Pit.
- **The sewers' three lost pipe rooms,** down the pipe Mario could only ever come out of, and the level-up's "Is this OK?" box.

*After the story*
- **The Paper Tower:** on a finished file, 30 floors of the game's corridor rooms with gates to get through in Paper or Tube mode, a Pit of 100 Trials battle every fifth floor, the day's climb for everyone or a seed of your own.
- **The trophy room:** a room under Rogueport that fills as the file plays: its Crystal Stars on the Door's pedestals, the bosses it has beaten as paper cut-outs, and a plaque for each achievement.
- **Area maps** (Enhancements): each area drawn from the game's own rooms and exits, with the rooms you've visited, the ways not yet taken and the Star Pieces and Shine Sprites still to find.
- **Daily shop specials** (Modes): five shops each put one rare item on a shelf at 30% off, a different one each day, the same for everyone.
- **35 more achievements** for the new places and modes, 60 in all, in every language.

*Looks*
- **Costumes:** paper versions of enemies' outfits (a Koopa's shell, a Boo's sheet, an X-Naut's hood and six more), made from the enemy's own model on your disc, unlocked by the file's Tattle Log.
- **Partner skins:** a character made with the character tools, drawn in a partner's place with its own animations.
- **Stickers:** partners' faces, items and enemies, stuck anywhere in the world from the Wardrobe as your file meets them.
- **The Wardrobe dresses every partner wherever they're drawn,** with new parts (Goombella's helmet, Flurrie's dress, Bobbery's fuse, Vivian's hat and hair, Ms. Mowz's cape).
- **HUD themes:** Night, Forest, Ocean, Candy or High contrast, for the HUD's banners and every Start menu page together.
- **Paper looks** (Graphics): sharp text at any resolution, smooth paper edges, paper shadows shaped like each character's cut-out, and paper physics (Subtle or Lively) that leans, flutters and squashes the cut-outs.
- **Two paper filters:** a cut-paper outline, and folding light.
- **Photo mode, in battles too:** nine filters, a soft or strong depth of field, and four frames. Pictures and F12 screenshots are saved at the window's size, with the effects on them, and the Journal has a Photos book to look through them.
- **Room transitions** (Graphics): doors and pipes turn the room away like a page, with the disc's own page animations.
- **The Tattle Log's models:** with an enemy's card open, the stick turns the enemy around, and L and R zoom in and out.
- **Widescreen bars that follow the camera,** sliding in only as the camera nears a room's open edge.

*Settings and controls*
- **Settings that make sense:** every setting now has one place, in groups under headings, the most used first, the same on the full page, in the launcher and in the Start menu. Play as has a group of its own, fast-forward sits with rewind, the jukebox under Audio, mirror mode with the story's other twists. What 0.4.0 added is marked "new".
- **Every row readable:** the Start menu's settings pages are in dark ink with a highlight band on the chosen row, as the game's own item lists, and every section of the full page has its page there.
- **Button icons:** Paper Cut's help lines and notices show the button that does each thing as its icon for the controller in your hands (GameCube, Xbox, PlayStation, Switch, Steam Deck) or as your key, following your bindings.
- **Touch controls:** a yellow C-stick (the free camera, the Tattle Log's model, battles' C-stick commands), an on-screen D-pad, a camera button for photo mode, a drag on the picture to turn the camera, the GameCube's colours and shapes (Controls > GameCube colours), and every control movable and resizable.
- **Soundtrack** (Audio): the game's music, your own, the game's shuffled, or yours and the game's shuffled, in one choice.
- **L and R step through the Start menu's tabs,** as left and right do on the tab row.
- **Bindings:** a second controller button for each of the game's buttons, keys with Ctrl, Shift, Alt or Cmd held for Paper Cut's actions, and named profiles, one of which can load by itself for a kind of controller.
- **The mouse camera:** with the free camera on, the mouse turns it too (desktop).
- **Partner wheel** (Enhancements, on by default): hold the partner quick-swap for a ring of the partners. A quick tap still swaps.
- **More haptics:** a tick as gauges and stick commands step on, and a pulse where they succeed.
- **Battery saver** (Display): on battery, or always, the picture drawn 30 times a second while the game still runs at 60.

*Saves, sound and Steam*
- **Every save file kept** (Saves, on by default): each save is also kept in a library, with a picture of where it was saved, and any of them goes back into one of the card's four files.
- **Settings per save file** (on by default): the difficulty, the Enhancements and the challenge rules saved with each file.
- **A copy for Dolphin or a GameCube** (Saves): a copy of your card with Paper Cut's own badges and dishes sold for their coins, readable by the original game.
- **Add to Steam** (General, Windows and Linux): Paper Cut in your Steam library as a non-Steam game, with artwork and controller set-ups.
- **Headphones (3D)** (Audio > Speakers): the game's surround mix on headphones, as if from speakers around your head.
- **Sound from the DualSense** (Audio, on by default): on a cable, the action commands' blips, the Superguard's clink and coins also play from the controller's speaker.

*Languages*
- **The games' own names:** the German, French, Spanish and Italian texts use the European release's names, and the Japanese text the Japanese release's, with the same words for the same settings throughout.
- **Every new feature in every language,** the achievements included.

*Mods*
- **A mod manager in the launcher:** a Mods tab with each mod's icon, name, version and author, a switch, drag to reorder, and conflicts shown. Drop a mod, a folder of mods, a language pack, a Dolphin texture pack or a `.lua` file on the window to install it. If the game closed while starting with mods on, the next start offers **safe mode**, every mod off.
- **Lua mods:** a mod can carry Lua scripts, with nothing to compile: the same mod runs on every platform and every version, in a sandbox of its own, with the game's events and an API for battles, maps, the HUD and challenge rules. The console is F4, and a mod in a folder reloads when its files change. Online, Lua mods run when every player has the same set.
- **Code mods need rebuilding:** a code mod built against 0.3.0's headers that reads the Start menu's `WindowWork` or the Journal's `WinLogState` must be built again for 0.4.0. Paper Cut's new items take numbers from 0x160 on. Mods of files, textures, text, data and scripts aren't affected.

*Elsewhere*
- **What's new:** the first start after an update shows this release's highlights on one screen; the full list is in the Start menu (Settings > General > What's new).
- **Install updates automatically** (General, off by default): a new release downloaded and checked in the background and put in place at the next start, never during online play or a replay.
- **Crash reports sent with a click:** a filled-in GitHub issue, or a zip to attach, with your name taken out of its paths.

**Fixed**
- Online, two players using different languages could fall out of step: drawing Paper Cut's own text took the game's random numbers. It never does now, wherever the text is drawn.
- Online, pausing, the window being put away, a change of game speed and the second screen's menu could put the games out of step. The game's clock is now read the same way in every game.
- Online, watchers and players 3 and 4 could drift out of step with the host from the start, reading the session's set-up from the wrong place.
- Online, the room closed when the player joining left or couldn't play, so the host had to send a new code.
- A key item that sank in water or fell through a floor could be lost for good. It now comes back where it appeared.
- A long translated or modded description in a battle's help window could run past its lines.
- In widescreen battles, the end of the audience count could show at the left edge.
- The Tattle Log's zoomed model was cut off by a box smaller than the card.
- The badge merchant's speech and help ran past their boxes.
- The built-in randomizer could move the Briefcase on the Excess Express, and then Zip Toad's scene couldn't be finished. It now stays where the game has it, so a seed shared from 0.3.0 places items differently.
- Character export refused about a hundred of the disc's models, Goombas and Koopas among them. Now 655 of the US disc's 656 come out and go back exactly.
- A memory card file made by Paper Cut had its empty files written in the PC's byte order, with checksums a GameCube or Dolphin wouldn't accept.
- A co-op replay could go out of step when played back with other controllers plugged in, or another window shape, than the recording had.
- A language pack's translation whose `%` codes differed from the English's could show the wrong text. It's now left out, and the pack's problems list says which.
- After a warp, a scene with a partner out where the story never has them showed a red "No messages". It now takes another partner's line.
- In widescreen, the battle hall's walls showed beside the stage, most of all as the curtain rose; at 21:9 and wider, some rooms showed the empty space past their walls; past 16:9, the title screen's side curtains stopped short of the edges.

**You need your own copy of the game:** a disc image (`.iso`, `.rvz`, `.gcm`...) made from your own disc. Paper Cut includes no game data. Add it in the launcher.

**Known issues**
- Paper Cut's relay is free, with a daily allowance shared by everyone: when it's used up, rooms, the room list, notes and boards say so until midnight UTC. Joining by the host's address still works then.
- **Phones and other devices are still to be checked.** The Android apps haven't run on a device, the second screen hasn't run on a real dual-screen device, and Linux and the Steam Deck haven't been played on. On phones the new touch C-stick and D-pad, the title menu and the launcher's stage are still to be tried at phone size and on high-density screens.
- The Start menu's Online tab isn't on phones and tablets, whose apps can't start themselves again: use the launcher there. Leaderboard runs are recorded on computers only.
- Online play has been tried mostly on one computer and one home network; between two homes, and with phones, it's still new. Watching a versus room, Lua mods with watchers, and the Westside's card players and stickers online are untried.
- Online, save states, fast-forward, frame advance, rewind, the Practice tab's editors, warps and the Twitch chat are off, and so are the dojo, photo mode, partner skins, paper notes and ghost races. The title menu is off during a session.
- Paper Cut's open world and the daily seed are the US version's only, and chapters 3 to 8 haven't been played through in it yet. Some things look odd: a partner found before their chapter shows in that chapter's scenes too; Rogueport's scenes follow how many chapters are done, so they may name another chapter's star; Keelhaul Key's wedding ring errand after chapter 5 doesn't play; and during General White's hunt the towns he's asked about show their later selves.
- For Peach and Bowser, collision, speed and jump are Mario's. Some of Peach's moves (lifting and throwing from behind, pushing, riding Flurrie, the Appeal) and the story's scenes with her are still to be played through.
- Three or four players through a room, and recording a replay with players 3 and 4, haven't been tried much yet.
- Much of 0.4.0 has been checked by scripted runs more than by hand: the Paper Tower's gates (Skip this floor moves on if one won't let Mario through), the quiz with four controllers, battle puzzles, Director's Cut's badges in battle, the save library's buttons, the area maps, the pings, the partner wheel, the mouse camera and the second controller buttons are still to be played properly.
- Some longer help notes take three lines: the window shows two, with an arrow for the rest.
- In mirror mode, signs and text that are part of the 3D world read backwards.
- Add to Steam has been tried against a test copy of Steam's folders, not yet a real Steam library or a Steam Deck. Headphones (3D) and surround haven't been listened to on real headphones or speakers, and the DualSense's speaker, haptics and light haven't been tried on a real controller.
- Read text aloud has only been run on Windows. Exclusive full screen hasn't been tried on a second monitor or a mixed-refresh setup.
- The translations are Paper Cut's own, not yet checked by native speakers.
- Nothing is code-signed. Windows SmartScreen warns on first run (More info > Run anyway). On macOS, the first time it's blocked: try to open it, then System Settings > Privacy & Security > Open Anyway. The iOS app must be signed by a sideloading tool.
