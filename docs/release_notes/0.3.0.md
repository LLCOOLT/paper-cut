Paper Cut 0.3.0 brings **online co-op**, **rewind** and **replays**, a **second screen** for dual-screen handhelds, the settings inside the game's own Start menu, and a long list of things to change and turn on: free camera, mirror mode, cosmetics, your own music, texture upscaling, accessibility options and more. It also fixes items in battle, which healed the wrong amounts. It plays the Japanese (G8MJ01), US (G8ME01) and European (G8MP01) versions of Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door, from the game's C decompilation, with no emulator.

**What's new**

*Playing together*
- **Online co-op:** the launcher's new Online tab. One player hosts and gives the room's code, the other joins with it: no router to set up. The two games connect directly when they can and through Paper Cut's relay when they can't, encrypted end to end with a key only the code's second half makes, so the relay sees nothing of the game. Connecting by address is still there. Both need Paper Cut and their own disc of the same release, on any platform: a PC can play with a Mac, a phone or a Steam Deck. Only the controllers' input crosses the network; both games play the same frames and check every second that they still match. The guest plays on the host's save and settings for the session, keeping their own display, sound and controls, and their own saves untouched.
- **Invites and watching:** an invite link (`papercut://join/<code>`), or a room code copied to the clipboard, joins with a click. Up to four more can join a room just to watch.
- **Staying connected:** after a network blip the two games reconnect and carry on. If they ever fall out of step, both start again together from the host's last save (up to three times a session).
- **Co-op in the field:** the second player's stick walks the partner anywhere; A hops, B talks or hits, X is the partner's ability, L or R switches partners. Mario calls the partner back with Y. In battle the second player has the partner's moves, action commands and guards, as before.
- **Joining is easy:** with co-op on, A on another controller joins as player 2. "1P" and "2P" show over the characters, a notice says when player 2 joins or leaves, and the Players section (Controls) shows who is who and picks player 2's controller with a press (a keyboard and one controller are enough).

*A second screen*
- On dual-screen handhelds like the AYN Thor (Android): the game's own Start menu, live on the lower screen while you play on the upper one, navigated by touch; using an item or switching partners from it works as in the menu. It appears only on devices with a second built-in screen.

*Settings*
- **In the Start menu:** a Settings tab in the game's own windows and font, its settings in categories as other games group them (Gameplay, Enhancements, Display, Graphics, Interface, Audio, Controls, Multiplayer, Accessibility, Language, General), each a page of its own, and All settings for the rest. The settings page uses the same categories.
- **Menu speed** (Interface): the Start menu's pages and tabs slide in half the time (Fast, the default) or at once (Instant).
- **Restart now** beside the settings that apply from the next start: the game starts again and puts you back where you were.
- **In six languages:** the settings, the launcher and the Start menu's tab in English, Deutsch, Français, Español, Italiano or Japanese (Language > Menu language).
- **Saves' tools while playing:** restoring a backup or copying a save to another release without quitting.
- **Display mode:** windowed, borderless or full screen (exclusive, at the screen's own resolution and refresh rate). F11 and Alt+Enter switch between the window and the full-screen kind you chose.
- **Settings presets** (General): your setup as a code or a file to share, and someone else's put in place. A preset holds the settings that change the game (gameplay, challenges, accessibility, cosmetics, mirror mode...), not the device's own (window, graphics, keys, controllers).

*Looks and sound*
- **Widescreen fixes:** the Start menu's backdrop, the page flip between rooms, and the title and file screens fill the width. Background pictures show more art instead of stretching. Bars go where a room's set or a battle's stage ends (Display > Bars where a scene ends).
- **Luigi's own face and name** when playing as Luigi: his head on the HUD, in the pause menu, on the BP coin and at level-up, made from his head on your disc, and "Luigi" on the pause menu's first tab.
- **Texture upscaling:** the game's textures 2× or 4× larger, through Paper Cut's own edge-following filter.
- **Free camera:** the right stick turns the camera a little around Mario and moves it in or out.
- **Mirror mode:** the world flipped left to right, the controls turned to match.
- **Photo mode:** P, both sticks pressed in, or its row in the Start menu's Settings tab. The game holds where it is; the sticks turn the camera all the way round Mario, up and down, in and out; Y hides the HUD and A takes the picture.
- **The Wardrobe:** a Start menu tab of its own where Mario, the partners and the HUD's bars are dressed while you watch them, in 13 colours or any from a picker, with Luigi, Wario, Waluigi and Fire Mario looks.
- **Music shuffle and your own music:** each room's music another of the game's; or your own OGG, MP3, FLAC or WAV files, named after the track they replace.
- **HDR output** on Windows and Mac displays that have it.
- **Surround** (Audio > Speakers): 5.1 or 7.1 from the game's own surround mix (made for Dolby Pro Logic II), sent to the rear speakers instead of folded into the front two.
- **The game plays in stereo:** until now Paper Cut played it in mono (a fresh GameCube's sound setting); its file-select choice is kept.
- **Volume:** sliders with mute buttons, and F9, F10 and F3 for volume anywhere.

*Controls*
- **Controllers:** the community mapping list (about 2,300 models), players 1 to 4, battery, a live test, dead zones, remapping for every player, and a set-up for controllers nothing knows. They're named as you know them ("DualSense", "Switch Pro Controller"), and so are their buttons.
- **Button prompts** that match your Xbox, PlayStation, Switch or Steam Deck controller, or your keys.
- **Steam Deck:** the first start sets itself up for the Deck's screen.
- **Controller haptics** (on by default): a short pulse as an action command's or a guard's window opens; on a DualSense the triggers resist while a command holds R.
- **One-handed controls:** left- or right-hand layouts for a player's controller, and holding a button instead of mashing it.
- **The controller's light** (Controls): on a DualSense, DualShock 4 or another controller with a coloured light, each player's colour, Mario's HP (green to red, pulsing when he's nearly out) or a colour of your own.

*Help and play*
- **Rewind** (Gameplay, off by default): hold Backspace, or a button you choose, and the game steps back through its last seconds (5 to 60).
- **Replays** (Practice): a run's input recorded to a small file and played back exactly, checked as it goes.
- **Faster battles** (1.5× or 2× while you're not acting; guards keep their time) and **slower game speed** (50% to 90%, or only in battle, for more time on the action commands).
- **Message log** (L): the last 200 pages of the message windows to scroll back through.
- **Colour-blind filters** (protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia, with a strength) and **captions for sounds:** a door, a switch, a hidden block, a guard, with an arrow when it's off screen.
- **Bigger text:** message windows up to 150%.
- **Read text aloud** (Accessibility): the system's voice reads the message windows page by page, and with "Messages and menus" the Start menu's help and the Settings tab as you move through them, in the game's language when the system has a voice for it.
- **"Where next?":** Y on the Journal tab names where the story goes next, and nothing is shown until you ask.
- **Challenges:** presets and four new rules (one-hit KO, no guarding, harder attack commands, no items in battle).
- **Boss refights:** any boss you've beaten, fought again.
- **Built-in randomizer** (US): a seed and what to shuffle, no server needed, with a spoiler log.
- **Autosave** at room changes (off by default), and a one-time note in the first room on where the settings are.
- **Input display** for streams and runs.

*Elsewhere*
- **Updates from the game** (Windows, macOS, the Linux zip): a new release is offered at the start, downloaded from Paper Cut's GitHub releases, checked against its published checksum, and put in place at the next start.
- **Save transfer:** saves sent between devices on your network by a QR code or an address, with a backup made first.
- **Language packs:** your own menu language, or changes to the built-in ones, from a folder in the mods folder, with a template to start from.
- **Discord status** (on by default): your profile shows the area you're in, the chapter you're on and Mario's level, and whether you're exploring, in battle, or playing co-op or online.
- **Linux:** an AppImage and a Flatpak beside the zip.
- **Save sync through any folder** on Windows and Linux (OneDrive, Dropbox, Google Drive, Syncthing...), as iCloud does on Apple devices.
- **Character export:** a character's model, animations and textures out of the game, edited in Blender, and back as a mod.
- **The same results on every computer:** the game now uses the GameCube's own maths library everywhere, which online play needs.

**Fixed**
- **Items in battle healed the wrong amounts** (in 0.2.0 too): every item restored 127 HP and no FP on Windows, and likely did nothing on the Mac and iPhone. They now heal what they say.
- A long session could freeze for good as a battle loaded.
- Loading a save in the field could freeze the game.
- Loading a save in the Glitz Pit wiped its fighter ranking.
- The 3D curtains, as in Cortez's fight, didn't cover the whole picture in widescreen.
- A mod's message with a stray `%` could crash a battle.
- Saves moved between Dolphin or a GameCube and Paper Cut read their play-time stamps backwards: sales ended at once, Trouble Center timers were off, and Yoshi's colour was random.
- A damaged shader cache crashed the start. It's now set aside and rebuilt.
- One of the crane action commands read the stick wrongly.

**You need your own copy of the game:** a disc image (`.iso`, `.rvz`, `.gcm`...). Add it in the launcher.

**Known issues**
- Online co-op has been played between a Mac and a Windows PC on the same home network, and through rooms on Paper Cut's own relay with a host, a guest and a watcher on one computer, in step throughout. Rooms between two homes, and with phones, are new. The relay is free and has a daily allowance shared by everyone it carries: when it's used up it says so until midnight UTC, and connecting directly or by address still works. Minimizing the window pauses that player's game, and the session ends after 15 seconds.
- Online, save states, fast-forward, frame advance, the Practice tab's editors, warps and the Twitch chat are off.
- The second screen hasn't run on a real dual-screen device yet. It shows the menu but doesn't equip badges, sort or change settings (the main screen's Start menu does), and in battle it keeps its last picture.
- The translations are Paper Cut's own, not yet checked by native speakers.
- In mirror mode, signs and text that are part of the 3D world read backwards.
- Read text aloud has only been run on Windows; the Mac, iPhone, Android and Linux voices are untried.
- The controller's light and the DualSense's haptics haven't been tried on a controller that has them.
- Exclusive full screen hasn't been tried on a second monitor or a mixed-refresh setup.
- Rewind, replays and the faster and slower speeds are off online.
- Surround hasn't been heard on real speakers: only its 6- and 8-channel output was measured.
- Photo mode works in the field only, not in battle and not online.
- Nothing is code-signed. Windows SmartScreen warns on first run (More info > Run anyway). On macOS, the first time it's blocked: try to open it, then System Settings > Privacy & Security > Open Anyway. The iOS app must be signed by a sideloading tool.
- Linux and the Steam Deck haven't been played on yet. The AppImage and the Flatpak are new and not yet tried.
