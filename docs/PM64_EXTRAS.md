# Paper Mario 64 extras

With your own Paper Mario (N64) ROM linked (Settings > Enhancements > Paper Mario 64 > Link Paper Mario 64), Paper
Cut can use more of it. Everything here is read from your ROM's cache in your user folder while you play;
nothing from the ROM is part of Paper Cut, and without a linked ROM none of it appears.

## The soundtrack

Settings > Audio > **Soundtrack** has two more choices:

- **Paper Mario 64's**: each place, battle and many scenes play a fitting Paper Mario song.
- **Paper Mario 64's and the game's, by area**: about half the areas (the same ones every time) play Paper
  Mario's songs, the rest keep the game's.

The songs are played by Paper Cut's own player for the N64 game's music (its sequences and instrument banks),
on the game's music channel, so the game's fades, pauses and music volume apply. Tracks with no match below
keep the game's music, and a file of yours in the music folder still wins over both. Only you hear it.

## Enemies

Settings > Enhancements > Paper Mario 64 > **Paper Mario 64 enemies**: in the Pit of 100 Trials and in Master Quest, about half
the battles have Paper Mario's own enemies in place of their Thousand-Year Door cousins, drawn as their sprites
from your ROM, with Paper Mario's HP and their names and Tattles from your ROM's text. Each fights with its
cousin's moves (a Paper Mario Koopa Troopa fights as a Koopa Troopa). Not online.

They are enemy variants, so the **Enemy maker** (Challenges) can make one too: *New Paper Mario 64 enemy*, then
change its numbers like any other; its code (`g=` in the code) works for anyone with a ROM linked.

Settings > Enhancements > Paper Mario 64 > **Tattle Log: Paper Mario 64** lists the ones you have met in battle (kept in
`pm64_tattles.txt` in the user folder) with your ROM's Tattle for each.

The Journal has a **Paper Mario** entry too (after Records): a book page for each of the enemies above, with
its sprite from your ROM, its HP, the enemy it fights as and its Tattle; the ones not met yet are a dark
outline.

**Tattle cards**: each Paper Mario enemy is a card as well, marked "(64)": its cousin's Attack and Defense
with Paper Mario's HP. Yours once you've met it in battle; the card players use them too. In an online duel
they're only in the draft when both players have a ROM linked.

### In the story

Settings > Enhancements > **Paper Mario 64 enemies in the story** (Off, Mixed, All; off at first): the
story's own fights can have Paper Mario's enemies, the ones that fit each area: Goombas, Paragoombas and
Spiked Goombas in Petal Meadows, Koopa Troopas and Fuzzies by the hills, Clefts in Boggly Woods, Hyper
Goombas and Dayzees on Twilight Trail, Embers and Lava Bubbles in Pirate's Grotto, Ruff Puffs and Pokeys by
Riverside Station, Frost Piranhas at Fahr Outpost, Dry Bones in the Palace of Shadow. Each takes the place of its Thousand-Year Door cousin from the table above.

Paper Mario's enemies with no cousin here join a fight beside its enemies, with their own Paper Mario moves:
Forest Fuzzies and Hyper Goombas in Boggly Woods, Hyper Paragoombas on Twilight Trail, Clubbas and
Duplighosts in Creepy Steeple, Spy Guys and Spear Guys on Keelhaul Key, Pyro Guys in Pirate's Grotto, Shy
Guys, Groove Guys and Spy Guys by Riverside Station, Clubbas at Fahr Outpost, Shy Guys and Medi Guys in the
X-Naut Fortress, Duplighosts in the Palace of Shadow.

- **Mixed**: two fights in five (the same ones every time: they're picked by the room and the enemy);
  **All**: every fight they fit. It applies from the next room.
- **In the field** the enemy whose fight is picked wanders as its Paper Mario sprite, with the game's own
  field behaviour (chasing, first strikes, stomps and hammer hits before the battle). Only enemies: the
  townsfolk (Toads, sailors, shopkeepers) always look like themselves, and each enemy becomes its own kind
  (a Koopa a Koopa, an Ember an Ember; Monty Mole and Shy Guy stand in only in the Pit and Master Quest).
- **In battle** each enemy of the fight with a Paper Mario cousin is that cousin: its sprite and its
  animations from your ROM (standing, walking, running, jumping, attacking, hurt, burnt, dizzy, asleep, in
  its shell), its name and Tattle from your ROM (the Tattle's HP and Attack are the ones it has here), and
  its cousin's moves (most are the same attacks in both games).
- **Its numbers**: Paper Mario's HP and attack, scaled to the Thousand-Year Door's chapter by chapter:
  HP = Paper Mario's HP x (a typical enemy's HP in this chapter / in its own Paper Mario chapter), rounded,
  and the same for the attack. A Goomba in Petal Meadows keeps 2 HP and 1 attack; a Dry Bones in the Palace
  of Shadow has 10 HP and 4 attack. Defence, statuses, coins, Star Points and items are the cousin's, so
  the area's rewards are the game's.
- **Left alone**: bosses, the story's scripted fights, the Glitz Pit's matches, the Pit of 100 Trials (it
  has the setting above) and the play modes' own fights.
- **Online and replays**: the host's setting applies, and only when every player has a ROM linked; the
  picks are the same in every game. A replay plays back the same where a ROM is linked.

## Bosses

Settings > Modes > **Paper Mario 64 bosses** (off at first): the boss rush ends with ten of Paper Mario's
bosses, and once the story is over each can be fought again from the Modes tab's **Boss refights**:
Jr. Troopa, the Koopa Bros., Tutankoopa, Tubba Blubba, General Guy, Lava Piranha, Huff N. Puff, the Crystal
King, Kammy Koopa and Bowser. Each is drawn as its sprite from your ROM, with its name and Tattle from your
ROM (Kammy, whom Paper Mario never fights, has a Tattle of Paper Cut's own), and fights with its own moves
made for Paper Cut from what each does in Paper Mario: jumps, rushes, shots, blows at the whole party,
quakes, heals and charges, some only once it's below half its HP.

In the story (**Paper Mario 64 bosses in the story**, on at first under it), each boss waits in Rogueport
once its Paper Mario partner's TTYD counterpart has joined (Jr. Troopa after Goombella, the Koopa Bros. after
Koops, their rematch after Bobbery, General Guy once chapter 4 is over; the Crystal King, Kammy Koopa and
Bowser near the end) and stays until it's beaten. With **Win Paper Mario partners from their bosses** (on at
first), beating a boss there or from the **Paper Mario 64 boss list** is how its partner joins you
([PM64_LINK.md](PM64_LINK.md#the-partners)); off, the partners join with their TTYD counterparts.

Walking into a boss in Rogueport (or talking to it) is an ordinary story battle, right there: Mario as he is, with
his own partners and items, no running away. Losing it is a Game Over, as with any of the game's own bosses. Jr.
Troopa, met in the prologue, fights as in his first Paper Mario fight (plain Jr. Troopa, a few HP); the boss list
and the board ask whether you want his first fight or his last one (his last, with all three forms, unless you say
otherwise).

How strong: a boss in Rogueport scales to Mario's level against its chapter in Paper Mario, from half of Paper
Mario's HP and attack for a low level up to exactly Paper Mario's numbers at that level or past it, never more.
From the boss list, the board or Boss refights it scales the same way with **Paper Mario 64 bosses: Scale to
your level** (on at first); off, and in the boss rush, they're as strong as the game's post-game: HP 2.5 times
Paper Mario's, each hit 2 more than Paper Mario's. Not online or in a replay.

## Badges

Settings > Enhancements > Paper Mario 64 > **Paper Mario 64 badges**: Rowf comes from Toad Town to the Star Haven corner of
Rogueport's plaza (at the end of the partners' back row) and sells Paper Mario badges the Thousand-Year Door hasn't got, for
coins. Their names, descriptions, BP and icons are your ROM's; Rowf is drawn as his sprite from it.

| Badge | What it does here |
| --- | --- |
| Attack FX A, B, C, D, E | the sound of Mario's attacks changes (TTYD's Attack FX R, G, B, Y, P) |
| Dizzy Stomp | a Jump that makes the enemy dizzy (it takes Sleepy Stomp's place in the menu while no Sleepy Stomp is worn) |
| Mega Jump, Mega Smash | Power Jump, Power Smash with one more power level (two of TTYD's badges in one) |
| Power Quake, Mega Quake | Quake Hammer with one or two more power levels |
| Triple Dip | three items in a turn (two Double Dips in one) |
| Damage Dodge | Mario's Defense goes up by 1 |
| Peekaboo | every enemy's HP shows, tattled or not |
| Pay-Off | after a battle won, a coin for each point of damage Mario took |

Online, they're only there when every player has a ROM linked. Without a ROM the badges you bought stay in
your bag with the game's look, and do nothing.

## Tayce T.'s recipes

Settings > Enhancements > **Tayce T.'s recipes**: Zess T. cooks Paper Mario's dishes too, from Paper Mario's
own recipes (one ingredient, two ingredients with the Cookbook, and Tayce T.'s dishes cooked with something
else). Their names, descriptions, icons and what they restore are your ROM's. Rowf, at the Star Haven corner of
Rogueport's plaza, comes for them as well and sells the Paper Mario ingredients the game hasn't got.

| Dish | Restores | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fried Shroom, Hot Shroom, Sweet Shroom | 6 HP 2 FP, 15 HP 5 FP, 30 HP 20 FP | |
| Bland Meal, Yummy Meal, Deluxe Feast | 10/10, 20/20, 40/40 | |
| Boiled Egg, Yoshi Cookie | 8/8, 15/15 | |
| Frozen Fries, Potato Salad | 15 HP, 10 HP | |
| Nutty Cake, Big Cookie, Special Shake, Healthy Juice | 10, 20, 20, 40 FP | |
| Lime Candy, Lemon Candy, Apple Pie | 20 FP, 5 HP 15 FP, 5 HP 15 FP | |
| Super Soda | 5 FP | cures as a Tasty Tonic does |
| Kooky Cookie | 15 FP | in battle electrifies, hides or puts to sleep, as a Love Pudding |
| Strange Cake | | the Kooky Cookie's effect; battle only |
| Egg Missile | | thrown as an Egg Bomb |

Rowf's ingredients: Iced Potato, Goomnut, Melon, Lime, Lemon, Apple, Strange Leaf and Stinky Herb (each is food
too, as in Paper Mario). Paper Mario's Koopa Leaf, Dried Pasta and Egg are the game's Turtley Leaf, Fresh Pasta
and Mystic Egg; its dishes the game already has (Koopa Tea, Spaghetti, Electro Pop, Fire Pop, the Honey, Maple
and Jelly Shrooms ...) are the game's own.

- A pair the game has its own recipe for stays the game's, except four, so that four dishes can be made at all:
  Mushroom + Dried Shroom (Fried Shroom), Super Shroom + Dried Shroom (Hot Shroom), Life Shroom + Cake Mix (Sweet
  Shroom), Fire Flower + Mystic Egg (Egg Missile). The game's dishes for those pairs have other recipes.
- Left out: the recipes that need a berry or Dried Fruit (what they make has other recipes), and the Coco Pop
  and the Jelly Pop, whose recipes are the game's Coco Candy's and Jelly Candy's.
- The Start menu's recipe book lists the dishes after the game's, with a hint for the ones not cooked yet. What
  each save file has cooked is kept beside it in your user folder (`Recipes/<game id>/file<n>.txt`, as the area
  maps are), so a new game starts a book of its own and the save itself stays the GameCube's.

Online, only when every player has a ROM linked. Without a ROM the dishes in your bag stay food, with the
game's look and their English names. As with Paper Cut's other items, a copy for Dolphin or a GameCube has them
sold (docs/SAVE_FILES.md).

## Small touches

- **Star Spirits** (Settings > Cosmetics > Paper Mario 64 Star Spirits, on): while Mario does a Special move,
  one of the seven Star Spirits looks on from the top of the screen, each Special its own.
- **Paper Mario 64 look** (the Start menu's Wardrobe, under Mario, with a ROM linked): Mario drawn as his Paper
  Mario sprite, in the field and in battle.

These read one more part of the ROM, the item icons, and the player's sprites (in the sprite section). Tayce T.'s
recipes read the item table too. A ROM linked with an earlier Paper Cut needs linking once more for them.
